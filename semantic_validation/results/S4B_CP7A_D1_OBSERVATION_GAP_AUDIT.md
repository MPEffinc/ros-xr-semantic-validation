## Method and exact evidence boundary

The new [read-only analyzer](runs/s4b_cp7a_observation_gap_20260926T123800Z/analysis/audit_gaps.py) joins the original receiver's `monitor_event_id` and original ROS payload SHA-256 to official monitor event, oracle property, official generated-monitor status, lossless-stripper callback, separate ROS recorder receipt and exact mapper callback. It audited **all ten B2 I_FULL trial arms**, not just the two invalid ones. Its [54-row event ledger](runs/s4b_cp7a_observation_gap_20260926T123800Z/analysis/unjoined_events.csv) contains each unjoined event ID, original payload hash, observed origin class, source ID (null), timing and boundary status. [Per-trial counts](runs/s4b_cp7a_observation_gap_20260926T123800Z/analysis/summary.json) retain the eight valid controls. Actual raw is the immutable `runs/s4b_d1_formal_20260926T104931Z/raw/<trial_id>/` tree: `lineage.jsonl`, `monitor_full_events.jsonl`, `property.jsonl`, `monitor_full_status.jsonl`, `topics.jsonl`, `events.jsonl`, `barrier.json`, `d1_nodes.ready`, participant clocks, stdout/stderr and `exit.json`. All source payload hashes in the join match their oracle rows.

The actual official generated `d1_full_guard.py` handles a topic event by writing the event, asking the oracle, then writing a `decision=forwarded` **status before** `_topic_callback` calls `publisher.publish(message)` (source lines 596–635 and 745–753 in `runs/s4b_cp5b_measurement_20260923T054101Z/monitor_ws/src/monitor/monitor/d1_full_guard.py`). Thus status is a forward *intent*, not a DDS publication or receipt ACK. The generated configuration `runs/s4b_cp6b_resource_q4_20260923T070559Z/inputs/d1_rosmonitoring.yaml` uses official ROSMonitoring `action: filter`, input `/s4b/d1/envelope_mon` and output `/s4b/d1/envelope`; `d1_nodes.py` creates both the receiver publisher and stripper subscriber at depth 20. The trial `events.jsonl` readiness graph confirms actual remap and downstream subscriber at the barrier. It does **not** prove they were connected for earlier neutral events. `topics.jsonl` is an additional recorder, not a complete upstream subscriber trace; absent recorder rows cannot prove no DDS publication.

For r02, the 53 oracle decisions span **4,940.224–4,094.106 ms before the start barrier** and **3,940.075–3,093.957 ms before the full readiness ACK**. `d1_nodes.ready` was written shortly before the first original publication, but that file is a process/node-construction marker, not a DDS match or subscriber processing ACK. The first stripper callback appears only after the 53-event burst. This is a startup-readiness association, **not** proof that ROSMonitoring deliberately dropped safe events. A depth-20 DDS queue, discovery/match delay, publisher call failure or callback scheduling are not distinguishable here without a direct post-publish/DDS receipt trace. Eight control arms and the other valid B2 I_FULL arm show 100% exact property/status/stripper joins (per-trial counts in `summary.json`); they do not retroactively repair r02.

For r05, the single event's oracle decision is **9,191.123 ms after barrier start** and **3,180.432 ms after recorder `capture_end`**. The probe's frozen source calls `node.destroy_node()`/`rclpy.shutdown()` immediately after the six-second capture loop (`inputs/pair_probe.py` in the Q4 root), while the receiver/monitor processes can still issue timeout-neutrals until the trial launcher exits. Its official monitor event and oracle decision are logged, but no generated-monitor status record follows; even the monitor's publish intent is not established. This is a distinct **capture/teardown lifecycle** gap. We cannot tell whether it was cancelled before status, published without a durable status, or lost during shutdown. A pre-barrier DDS explanation cannot explain this post-capture event.

`r02` and `r05` sent all 120 source fixture indices and had normal active-input and Gazebo positive control. The 54 unjoined events have no source sample ID, are original receiver timeout-neutrals, and cannot be counted as sender sample false rejects. There is no exact per-neutral Servo-output or Gazebo-joint parent; remaining interval movement is not assigned to these events. No monitor policy failure, robot hazard or actual Quest phenomenon is established.

## Remedy classification and D1 decision

The two gaps require **different mechanisms**. r05 suggests an observation-lifecycle correction (CLASS A candidate: stop admitting/property-logging only after an ACKed, quiescent monitor/receiver shutdown while keeping the recorder alive), but the exact final monitor callback fate remains unknown. r02 has the more consequential **CLASS C unresolved root cause**: the source logs do not distinguish official ROS publish failure, DDS transport loss, and early stripper readiness. Moving receiver startup after an ACKed downstream DDS match is a plausible **CLASS B integration change**, not a proven observation-only repair; it could affect control-path scheduling. There is therefore **no single causally established observation-only correction** covering both incidents. CP7 does not authorize an outcome-seeking D1 rerun or a new campaign on this evidence alone. D1 retains its original PARTIAL 48/50 result; no supplemental trial was preregistered or run. A future prospective integration variant would need direct publish/receipt observation, ACKed startup and graceful quiescence, a new freeze, B0/shim equivalence and separate reporting—not automatic pooling with the original 48.

This classification distinguishes integration/observation uncertainty from a functional defense failure. Official ROSMonitoring source and semantics were not modified. No result window was extended post hoc and no neutral event was excluded from frozen completeness scoring. D2/D3 preparation proceeds independently under unchanged case definitions; this audit does not qualify those cases.
